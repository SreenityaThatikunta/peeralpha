Reading the numbers one formula is not the model

IC is the daily cross-sectional correlation between a formula's values and each stock's next 5-day return, averaged over days. On liquid large caps, a single formula with a steady IC of 0.02–0.04 is good.

Sign does not matter much. A formula with a steady negative IC is just as useful: the pool's ridge weights flip it. Within-sector reversal is the classic example.

The model is a pool. PeerAlpha keeps about 10 formulas and combines them with ridge regression. The headline numbers on the Results page are for those pools, averaged over 3 seeds, so they are higher than most single formulas here.

t is a Newey-West t-statistic, which allows for the overlap between neighbouring days' 5-day returns.

The problem

Quant funds rank stocks with alpha factors: formulas over price and volume that predict which stocks will do better than others over the next few days. Finding good ones by hand is slow, so recent work searches for them automatically.

One line of work, starting with AlphaGen, trains a reinforcement-learning agent to write formulas token by token, like Corr($close, $volume, 20), and rewards it when a new formula improves a combined pool of formulas. The result is a short list of formulas a person can read and check, rather than a black-box model.

What those formulas cannot see

In AlphaGen and its successors, every operator works on one stock's own history, plus cross-sectional ranks over the whole market. But a lot of what moves a stock comes from the stocks around it:

● Sector momentum

When a stock's industry has been rising, the stock tends to keep rising with it.

● Peer reversal

A stock that jumped far ahead of its peers this week often gives some of it back.

● Lead-lag

Some stocks react a day after others, so yesterday's move in a leader says something about a follower today.

A formula that only looks at one stock at a time cannot express "my return minus my sector's average return".

What PeerAlpha asks

  • Can the agent use peers? Add graph operators to the formula language that read a stock's neighbours on a relationship graph.
  • Does it help out of sample? Compare against the same agent without graph operators, random search, and hand-built peer factors.
  • Is the result real? Walk-forward tests over 2018–2024, several seeds, paired tests and Newey-West t-statistics, with every choice made on validation data.

The data

Daily prices for Indian large caps from Yahoo Finance, 2011–2024: the Nifty 200 constituents with full history (145 stocks, 18 NSE industries) and an earlier universe of about 100 NSE stocks. The playground on this site runs on the Nifty 200 set.

How it works

A MaskablePPO agent writes formulas in postfix, one token per step. Finished formulas join a pool, and the agent is paid for how much each one improves the pool out of sample.

1 · data

Daily prices

Returns, 12-1 momentum, volatility, close vs VWAP, day range.

2 · graphs

Peer graphs

Sector, correlation and lead-lag neighbours, built only from past data.

3 · agent

MaskablePPO

A graph-attention policy over the partial formula tree picks the next token.

4 · pool

Alpha pool

About 10 formulas, ridge-combined. Reward: the out-of-fold IC gain.

5 · test

Walk-forward

Three test periods, 3 seeds, paired tests against controls.

The decision problem

StateThe partial formula: its tokens, the operator-to-operand edges of its tree, the open subtrees on the stack, and the pool's state (budget left, fill, current IC).
ActionThe next token: a feature, constant, window, graph, operator, or end-of-formula. A postfix grammar builds an action mask so only tokens that keep the formula valid and finishable within 10 tokens can be picked.
RewardZero until the formula ends. Then the gain in the pool's out-of-fold IC when the formula joins, plus a small diversity bonus. Invalid, duplicate or sign-unstable formulas are penalised.
PolicyA graph-attention encoder over the formula tree (an LSTM baseline is included), trained with MaskablePPO from sb3-contrib.

Graph operators

Each takes a series and a graph, and for every stock and day it looks at that stock's neighbours on that day. Values are z-scored across stocks first, so peers are compared on one scale.

NbrMeanMean over the neighbours. NbrMean($ret20, G_sector) is sector momentum.
NbrRelOwn value minus the neighbour mean. NbrRel($ret5, G_sector) is how far a stock ran ahead of its peers this week.
NbrRankShare of neighbours whose value is below the stock's own.
NbrStd, NbrMax, DiffuseSpread, maximum, and a two-hop mean. In the code, but left out of the final vocabulary.

All graphs are causal: the graph used on day t is built from data up to day t−1. The sector graph links same-industry stocks, ranked by how closely their sector-adjusted returns move together in the training years.

Keeping the pool honest

The first versions overfit badly: training IC of 0.12–0.18 against test IC near 0.02. The fixes that mattered:

  • Out-of-fold reward. A formula's gain is measured on folds of the training data it was not fitted on.
  • Sign gate. Formulas whose IC flips sign across training folds are rejected.
  • A small pool of 10 members with ridge regularisation, and a swap only when it raises the pool's score.
  • A hand-built prior. The pool starts with sector momentum, within-sector reversal and 12-1 month momentum as fixed members, so the agent is paid only for what it adds on top of them.

Evaluation protocol

Expanding windows: train from 2012, validate on the two years before each test period, then test on 2018–19, 2020–21 and 2022 to mid-2024. Every learned method runs 3 seeds per window. Comparisons are paired by seed and window. Blend weights are picked on validation, never on test.

Stack

Python, PyTorch, Stable-Baselines3 and sb3-contrib for MaskablePPO, NumPy and pandas. Training runs on Kaggle CPU kernels, one per walk-forward window. This page's formula lab is a JavaScript port of the formula grammar, operators and IC code; it matches the Python package's IC to within 1e-5 on the formulas we checked (apart from tie order inside CsRank).

Reading the results honestly

  • Peer information is real signal. Graph operators beat the no-graph agent in every seed and period, on both universes.
  • The hand-built factor is very hard to beat. Sector momentum, within-sector reversal and 12-1 momentum together reach 0.047. Learned pools get close, and blending adds about 0.003 on Nifty 200, which is not significant. On 100 stocks the blend ties.
  • PPO and random search score about the same with the same vocabulary and prior (0.045 vs 0.044). The formula language and the pool design matter more than the search method.
  • Where learned pools do win is long-short portfolios: Sharpe about 1.5 against 1.2.
  • Next: more seeds and periods on Nifty 200 to tell whether the blend's edge is real, and scoring pools on long-short returns as well as IC.

Run it yourself

git clone https://github.com/SreenityaThatikunta/peeralpha.git && cd peeralpha
python -m venv .venv && source .venv/bin/activate
pip install -r requirements.txt
python scripts/fetch_nse.py --universe_csv ind_nifty200list.csv --out data/nifty200.npz
python scripts/walk_forward.py --data data/nifty200.npz --tag v5n200 --seeds 0 1 2 --valid_years 2 \
  --methods v5_sector_prior v4_sector v4_nograph v5_random_prior --steps 300000 \
  --baseline v4_nograph --hand --jobs 4 --threads 1
